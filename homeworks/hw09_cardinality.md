# Homework09: Cardinality

Let $\mathcal{P}(A)$ denote the power set of $A$.

## Exercise 1 (10 points)

Assume an English text is written using the 26 letters, a space, and a finite set of punctuation marks. Determine whether each statement is true or false. Justify your answers.

1. The set of all English texts of maximum length 500 is countable.
2. The set of all English texts of finite length is uncountably infinite.

## Exercise 2 (33 points)

For each pair of sets, describe an explicit bijection from the first set to the second. You do not need to prove that your function is bijective. Each part is worth 3 points.

1. $\mathbb{R}$ and $(0, \infty)$.
2. $\mathbb{R}$ and $(\sqrt{2}, \infty)$.
3. $\mathbb{R}$ and $(0, 1)$.
4. $\mathbb{Z}$ and $S = \lbrace \ldots, 1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, \ldots \rbrace$.
5. $A = \lbrace 3k : k \in \mathbb{Z} \rbrace$ and $B = \lbrace 7k : k \in \mathbb{Z} \rbrace$.
6. $A = \lbrace (5n, -3n) : n \in \mathbb{Z} \rbrace$ and $\mathbb{Z}$.
7. The set of even integers and the set of odd integers.
8. $\mathbb{Z}$ and $S = \lbrace x \in \mathbb{R} : \sin(x) = 1 \rbrace$.
9. $\lbrace 0, 1 \rbrace \times \mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{N}$.
10. $\mathbb{N}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$.
11. $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ and $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{Z})$.

## Exercise 3 (12 points)

Let $S = (0, 1)$.

1. Find a bijection from $S$ to $U = (0, 2)$. You do not need to prove that it is bijective. (6 points)
2. Prove that $S$ and $V = (2, 5)$ have the same cardinality. (6 points)

## Exercise 4 (15 points)

1. Prove that if $A$ and $B$ are countable, then $A \cup B$ is countable. (7 points)
2. Use part 1 to prove that the set of irrational numbers is uncountable. You may assume that $\mathbb{Q}$ is countable and $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable. (8 points)

## Exercise 5 (20 points)

A real number is **algebraic** if it is a root of a nonzero polynomial in one variable with integer coefficients. For example, $(1 + \sqrt{5})/2$ is algebraic because it is a root of $x^2 - x - 1$. Every rational number $p/q$, where $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$ and $q \ne 0$, is algebraic because it is a root of $qx - p$.

Complete the following steps to prove that non-algebraic real numbers exist. You may use earlier parts in later parts.

1. Prove that if $A$ and $B$ are countable, then $A \times B$ is countable. (4 points)
2. Prove that a countable union of countable sets is countable. (6 points)
3. Prove that the set of polynomials in one variable with integer coefficients is countable. (5 points)
4. Prove that there are real numbers that are not algebraic. You may use the facts that $\mathbb{R}$ is uncountable and that a nonzero polynomial of degree $n \geq 1$ has at most $n$ real roots. (5 points)

## Exercise 6 (10 points)

1. Let $A$ be a finite set with $n$ elements. How many elements does $\mathcal{P}(A)$ have? Justify your answer. (3 points)
2. Is $\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})$ countable? Prove your answer. (7 points)
