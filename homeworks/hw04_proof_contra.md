# Homework04: Proof by Contraposition and Contradiction

## Exercise 1 (15 points)

Use proof by contraposition to prove that for any integers $a$ and $b$, if $a^2 + b^2$ is divisible by $4$, then both $a$ and $b$ are even.

## Exercise 2 (15 points)

Use proof by contraposition to prove that for any integer $n$, if $n^2$ is divisible by $3$, then $n$ is divisible by $3$.

## Exercise 3 (15 points)

Use proof by contraposition to prove that for any integers $a$ and $b$, if $a + b + ab$ is even, then both $a$ and $b$ are even.

## Exercise 4 (15 points)

Use proof by contradiction to prove that there are no integers $x$ and $y$ such that $x^2 - y^2 = 2$.

## Exercise 5 (15 points)

Use proof by contradiction to prove that for every positive integer $n$, $n^2 + n + 1$ is not a perfect square.

## Exercise 6 (25 points)

Use proof by contradiction to prove that there are no positive integers $a$ and $b$ such that $a^2 = 2b^2$.

**Hint:** You may use the well-ordering principle: every nonempty set of positive integers has a least element. If a solution exists, consider a solution with the smallest possible value of $b$.
