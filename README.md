# CSE 113: Foundations of Computer Science (SUNY Korea, Fall 2026)

## Course Overview

This course is offered by the Computer Science Department at SUNY Korea. It
covers discrete mathematics, a fundamental branch of mathematics that is
essential for a deep understanding of computer science. Topics include
propositional and predicate logic, number theory, proof techniques, sequences,
recursion, functions, relations, and sets.

The course materials in this public repository will be added throughout the
semester. We will adapt materials previously used at SUNY Korea and Stony
Brook University for this curriculum.

## Course Objectives

By the end of this course, students should be able to:

- Create truth tables for various applications, such as verifying the validity
  of an argument or determining the equivalence of two propositions.
- Reason precisely and prove mathematical theorems.
- Demonstrate a basic understanding of mathematical concepts such as
  sequences, functions, and relations.

## Definition of Force Majeure in Our Policies

Force majeure in our policies includes, but is not limited to, documented
illness and family emergencies.

Note: A documented hospital visit alone is not sufficient to justify force
majeure. The doctor's note must clearly state the reason for the absence and
the recommended period of absence.

## Attendance Policy

- Attendance will be checked irregularly.
- Arriving late will count as half attendance. "Late" means arriving after
  the attendance check has been completed. After the attendance check, the TA
  or instructor will record any absences.
- Excuses will be granted only in cases of force majeure. See the definition
  above for "force majeure."
- If there is a discrepancy between the signed attendance sheet and a
  student's actual physical presence, names will be called. The students
  involved may be addressed privately.
- In accordance with Korean law, an absence rate of more than 20% will result
  in an automatic F grade.

## Grading Policy

- Both the TA and the instructor will grade assignments.
- Factual errors in grades will be corrected, but nonfactual grade disputes
  will not be considered.
- Plagiarism, including the use of AI-generated solutions for homework, will
  result in a grade of 0 and will be reported. The students involved may be
  addressed privately.
- Typed submissions are recommended. Illegible handwriting may result in a
  grade of 0.

## Late Homework Policy

Late homework is not accepted except in documented cases of force majeure.

## LLM Policy

We follow policies similar to those outlined in [Stanford's Generative AI
Policy Guidance](https://communitystandards.stanford.edu/generative-ai-policy-guidance).

- In general, using or consulting generative AI is treated similarly to
  receiving assistance from another person.
- Using generative AI tools such as ChatGPT to substantially complete homework
  assignments is not permitted.
- Students should acknowledge any use of generative AI tools beyond incidental
  use and should disclose such assistance when in doubt.
